# Self-Regulation Activities

This document explains the basis for the two types of activity MKpause offers students during a session: **breathing exercises** and **casual games**. The sources are two systematic reviews: Bentley et al. (2023), on breathing practices, and Pine et al. (2020), on casual games.

> A **systematic review** gathers and evaluates all published studies on a question, following a method defined before the search begins. It therefore gives a broader view than any single study.

---

## Guided breathing

### What the research says

Bentley et al. (2023) analyzed 58 studies, covering 72 breathing interventions and 5,407 participants, to understand which practices reduce stress and anxiety.

Main findings:

- **54 of the 72 interventions** were effective in reducing stress or anxiety;
- in the studies with **young people** (four randomized controlled trials) and with highly anxious adults, all interventions analyzed were effective;
- learning to control one's breathing may give a **sense of control** over one's own state.

Based on these findings, the authors proposed implementation guidelines:

| Guideline | Description |
|---|---|
| Avoid fast breathing alone | Do not rely solely on fast-paced breathing techniques |
| Avoid very short sessions | Do not use sessions shorter than 5 minutes |
| Human guidance | Include training led by a person, considered a core component |
| Multiple sessions | Repeat the practice rather than relying on a single session |
| Long-term practice | Six or more sessions over at least one week |

The authors also point out that the methodological quality of most studies ranged from fair to poor. The results are promising but should be interpreted with caution.

### How the research applies

**Slow pace.** In PianoPause, guided breathing follows an 8-second cycle: 4 seconds to breathe in and 4 to breathe out. That is 7.5 breaths per minute, a slow pace, consistent with the guideline against relying on fast breathing alone.

**Sense of control.** The circle that expands and contracts gives students a visual reference for following their own breathing, at their own pace and with no score.

**Repeated practice.** The session history (RF09) lets students return to the practice over time, which moves toward the multiple-sessions guideline.

**Human guidance.** The platform does not replace professionals (RN36). Links with teachers and psychologists, along with the alert system, keep people involved in the process, which the review identified as a core component.

### Points of attention

- A PianoPause session lasts **2 minutes**, below the 5 minutes indicated by the review. A longer guided-breathing option, chosen by the student, is worth evaluating.
- The effectiveness observed in studies with young people comes from only four trials, a small number.

## Casual games

### What the research says

Pine et al. (2020) reviewed 13 studies on **casual games**: simple games that are easy to learn and played in short sessions. Nine different games were evaluated. Six studies addressed anxiety, two addressed depression and four addressed stress or low mood.

The authors concluded that the results are **promising** for anxiety, stress and low mood. Because the number of studies is still small, more research is needed to confirm these effects.

### How the research applies

PianoPause was built with the characteristics of a casual game:

| Casual game characteristic | In PianoPause |
|---|---|
| Simple rules | Play the keys that move down the screen |
| Immediate learning | No tutorial; the action is intuitive |
| Short sessions | The session ends after 2 minutes or whenever the student chooses to stop |

In addition, the game has **no score, mistakes, losing or visible countdown**. A key that is not played simply fades away. The absence of penalties keeps the activity from becoming one more source of pressure at school.

### Guidelines for new games

1. Rules that can be understood in a few seconds.
2. Short duration, with the option to stop at any time.
3. No competitive scoring, rankings or penalties for mistakes.
4. Use of the platform's [palette](./paleta-de-cores.en.md) and [rounded shapes](./formas-e-mascote.en.md).
5. Integration with mood recording before and after the activity (RF04 and RF06), so the platform can track the effect the student perceives.
